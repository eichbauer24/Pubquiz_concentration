Hard questions: fact bank

NarrenCastl writes for a European audience, so expect a mix of science, pop culture, geography and history. These are the facts most likely to surface.

Chemical concentration
  • Molarity c = n/V in mol/L (symbol M). Molality is mol per kg of solvent and doesn't change with temperature.
  • Mass concentration in g/L; ppm = parts per million; ‰ = per mille.
  • Dilution: c₁·V₁ = c₂·V₂.
  • pH = −log₁₀ of the H₃O⁺ concentration. pH 7 neutral; each step is a factor of 10.
  • Beer–Lambert law: absorbance A = ε·c·d. It's how photometers measure concentration.
  • Avogadro constant: 6.02214076 × 10²³ per mol, exact since the 2019 SI redefinition.
  • Seawater: about 35 g salt per kg (3.5%). The Dead Sea is roughly ten times saltier.
  • CO₂ in air: over 420 ppm today, versus about 280 ppm before industrialisation.
  • Uranium: natural 0.7% U-235, reactor fuel 3–5%, weapons grade about 90%. "Yellowcake" is uranium ore concentrate.
  • Blood alcohol limit in Austria: 0.5‰, and 0.1‰ for probationary drivers.
  • Osmosis: water moves toward the higher solute concentration.
  • Concentrated sulfuric acid is about 98%. Always add acid to water, never water to acid.
Focus in optics, physics and geology
  • Latin focus means hearth or fireplace. Kepler brought it into optics: the point where sunlight burns. Plural: foci.
  • German Brennpunkt literally means "burning point".
  • Diopter = 1 / focal length in metres. A +2 dpt lens has f = 50 cm.
  • f-number = focal length ÷ aperture diameter. A smaller f-number means a bigger opening and shallower depth of field.
  • Bokeh comes from Japanese boke, meaning blur or haze.
  • Ellipse: two foci. Kepler's first law puts the Sun at one focus of each planet's orbit.
  • Earthquakes: the focus (hypocentre) is underground; the epicentre is the point directly above it on the surface.
  • Eye: the lens changes shape to focus (accommodation). Myopia = short-sighted, hyperopia = long-sighted, presbyopia = age-related loss of near focus.
  • First mass-produced autofocus camera: Konica C35 AF, November 1977, using Honeywell's "Visitronic" system.
  • First SLR with autofocus built into the body: Minolta 7000 (1985).
  • Hubble telescope launched in 1990 with a flawed mirror that couldn't focus sharply; astronauts fixed it in 1993.
  • Leica comes from Wetzlar (Leitz + camera); Carl Zeiss was founded in Jena in 1846.

Mautner Markhof and concentrates
  • Founded 1841 in Vienna by Adolf Ignaz Mautner, originally as the St. Marx brewery.
  • Mautner Markhof is credited with inventing industrial press yeast (the "Viennese process", 1846).
  • Known for Kremser Senf (sweet mustard), Estragon Senf, Hesperiden Essig (1927) and syrups.
  • First syrup launched in the early 1930s. Based in Vienna-Simmering.
  • Owned by the Munich mustard maker Develey since the early 2000s.
  • A typical syrup is diluted about 1 part syrup to 7–10 parts water. Juice "from concentrate" has water removed for transport and added back later.
  • Tomato purée: doppelt/dreifach konzentriert (double/triple concentrated).

Psychology of attention
  • Stroop effect (J. Ridley Stroop, 1935): naming the ink colour of a colour word is slow when they don't match.
  • d2 test (Rolf Brickenkamp, 1962): cross out every "d" with two dashes. Originally for driver assessment. 14 rows × 47 characters = 658.
  • Cocktail party effect (Colin Cherry, 1953): focusing on one voice among many.
  • Invisible gorilla (Simons and Chabris, 1999): about half of viewers counting basketball passes miss a person in a gorilla suit. Ig Nobel Prize 2004.
  • Flow: Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi's state of total absorption.
  • Pomodoro: Francesco Cirillo, late 1980s. 25 minutes work, 5 minutes break; pomodoro is Italian for tomato, after his kitchen timer.
  • Yerkes–Dodson law (1908): performance peaks at medium arousal.
  • Kim's game (memorise objects on a tray) comes from Rudyard Kipling's novel Kim (1901).
  • The "goldfish attention span of 8 seconds" is a myth.
  • Caffeine keeps you alert by blocking adenosine receptors.
Concentration games and sport

  • Concentration was also a long-running US TV game show on NBC (1958–1973).
  • Biathlon: five targets per shoot; prone targets 4.5 cm, standing 11.5 cm; a miss usually means a 150 m penalty loop.
  • Darts: maximum score 180; board centre 1.73 m high; throw line 2.37 m away; games from 501.
  • Snooker: maximum break 147. Ronnie O'Sullivan's fastest 147 took 5 min 8 s (1997).
  • Chess: Deep Blue beat Kasparov in 1997.
  • Other steady-hand games: Jenga, Operation, Mikado.

Final round

Past finals were hands-on tasks built around the theme, on paper. For concentration, these are the likely formats. Try the drills below.

  • d2-style crossing out under time pressure
  • Letter or symbol counting in a paragraph or grid
  • Number search: find 1 to 30 (or more) in order on a scattered sheet
  • Stroop table: write down ink colours, not words
  • Spot the difference, hidden objects, error hunt in a text
  • Memory: a picture shown briefly, then questions
  • Dilution calculations (c₁V₁ = c₂V₂), your specialty

Drill 1: d2 crossing out

Tap every d with exactly two dashes (above and below combined). You have 20 seconds.

Drill 2: number search

Tap 1 to 30 in order as fast as you can.

Drill 3: Stroop

Say the ink colour of each word out loud, left to right. Time yourself, then shuffle and beat it.

Drill 4: letter count

Count the letter F. Read once, no going back.

Team tactics

  • Assign specialists before the quiz: science and chemistry (you), pop culture and TV, music, sport and cars.
  • Music round with mixed songs: each person follows one layer: vocals, bassline, melody.
  • Paper tasks: split the sheet, then swap and check. Use a card or ruler to cover finished rows.
  • Mark systematically: strike through each counted item so nothing gets counted twice.
  • First instinct stands unless someone has a concrete reason. Never leave a blank.
  • Answers in German are allowed. Use whichever language you're surer of.
  • One writer holds the sheet and has the final say, so debates don't eat the time.
